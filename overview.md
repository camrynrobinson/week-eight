# Week Eight: Space Syntax & Practical Necromancy

### Space Syntax
- Frequently, floor plans are all that remains of a site when it comes to the attention of archaeologists. The bottoms of walls, the outlines of rooms or gardens or roads, provide a evidence which can be combined with a series of theoretical statements/assumptions about certain behaviours and relationships associated with certain environments (space syntax) to interpret their historical meaning.

### Neural Networks
- LLMs are not sentient, nor are they original. they are ghostly, generated apparitions of echoed, stolen data from an incomprehensible diversity of sources, averaging and connecting and re-averaging the information to produce the "ideal" answer (attention model) - not necessarily the right one. LLMs are a subset of machine learning algorithms, which can, when applied responsibly, be incredibly useful in archaeology for their ability to identify patterns across massive datasets. there are two broad categories of machine learning: supervised (labeled data to predict outcomes) and unsupervised (unlabeled data to find patterns); supervised learning takes more time and effort, but unsupervised learning is susceptible to noise interference from messy data (essentially unavoidable in archaeology) which means time saved not having to monitor the process is expended instead on scrutinizing the results.
